SCOPE AND CONTRIBUTION

Clinical communication — particularly the anamnesis encounter — constitutes
one of the richest yet least analytically exploited data sources in healthcare.
Recent advances in Automatic Speech Recognition (ASR), Natural Language
Processing (NLP) and vocal biomarker analysis have expanded the technical
capacity to extract structured information from spoken clinical interactions.
Despite this progress, few studies operationalise the clinical interaction
itself as a source of speech-derived measurable variables, particularly
communicational and behavioural markers that can inform adherence monitoring
and care quality assessment.

This scoping review systematically maps the evidence on Speech Analytics and
NLP applied to clinical communication (2014–2026), following PRISMA-ScR
guidelines (Tricco et al., 2018) and JBI methodology (Peters et al., 2020).
The corpus of 24 included studies — drawn from PubMed/MEDLINE and supplemented
by backward snowballing — was organised into four thematic axes: (A) clinical
NLP and biomedical entity extraction; (B) automated clinical documentation and
ASR; (C) physician-patient communication; and (D) vocal biomarkers and
multimodal analysis. The review confirms a consistent gap between the
documented technical capabilities and the operationalisation of the anamnesis
as a structured analytical unit, and proposes a research agenda for prospective
studies integrating Speech Analytics with structured clinical communication
analysis.

WHY IJMI

The International Journal of Medical Informatics is the primary venue for
this work: the four thematic axes span NLP-based clinical information
extraction, ASR-driven documentation systems, and computational approaches
to clinical communication — all central to IJMI's scope. The confirmed gap
identified (clinical interactions rarely operationalised as speech-derived
variables) maps directly onto the journal's longstanding interest in the
clinical translational challenges of health data science.
